# 1: An element lies in its own equivalence class
## Proof that
### then a is in the class of a

1. Let X be any set, R be an equivalence relation on X, and a be in X. (Declaration)
2. exists C, forall u, (In(u, C) iff (In(u, X) and R(a, u))). (Separation)
3. Let C be such a set. (Existence from 2)
4. R(a, a). (Relation Reflexivity from 1)
5. In(a, X) and R(a, a). (Conjunction Introduction from 1, 4)
6. In(a, C). (Set property, 3, 5)

# 2: Related elements have nested classes
## Proof that
### if R(a, b) then the class of a is a subset of the class of b

1. Let X be any set, R be an equivalence relation on X, and a, b be in X. (Declaration)
2. R(a, b). (Premise)
3. exists C, forall u, (In(u, C) iff (In(u, X) and R(a, u))). (Separation)
4. Let C be such a set. (Existence from 3)
5. exists D, forall u, (In(u, D) iff (In(u, X) and R(b, u))). (Separation)
6. Let D be such a set. (Existence from 5)
7. C subseteq D. (Subset proof below)
 begin subproof
  7.1. Let u in C. (Assumption for subset proof)
  7.2. In(u, C) iff (In(u, X) and R(a, u)). (Universal Instantiation from 4)
  7.3. In(u, C) -> (In(u, X) and R(a, u)). (Biconditional Elimination from 7.2)
  7.4. In(u, X) and R(a, u). (Modus Ponens from 7.3, 7.1)
  7.5. R(a, u). (Conjunction Elimination from 7.4)
  7.6. R(b, a). (Relation Symmetry from 2)
  7.7. R(b, u). (Relation Transitivity from 7.6, 7.5)
  7.8. In(u, X). (Conjunction Elimination from 7.4)
  7.9. In(u, X) and R(b, u). (Conjunction Introduction from 7.8, 7.7)
  7.10. In(u, D) iff (In(u, X) and R(b, u)). (Universal Instantiation from 6)
  7.11. (In(u, X) and R(b, u)) -> In(u, D). (Biconditional Elimination from 7.10)
  7.12. In(u, D). (Modus Ponens from 7.11, 7.9)
 end subproof
