# 1: An element lies in its own equivalence class
## Proof that
### then a is in the class of a

1. Let X be any set, R be an equivalence relation on X, and a be in X. (Declaration)
2. exists C, forall u, (In(u, C) iff (In(u, X) and R(a, u))). (Separation)
3. Let C be such a set. (Existence from 2)
4. R(a, a). (Relation Reflexivity from 1)
5. In(a, X) and R(a, a). (Conjunction Introduction from 1, 4)
6. In(a, C). (Set property, 3, 5)

# 2: Related elements have nested classes
## Proof that
### if R(a, b) then the class of a is a subset of the class of b

1. Let X be any set, R be an equivalence relation on X, and a, b be in X. (Declaration)
2. R(a, b). (Premise)
3. exists C, forall u, (In(u, C) iff (In(u, X) and R(a, u))). (Separation)
4. Let C be such a set. (Existence from 3)
5. exists D, forall u, (In(u, D) iff (In(u, X) and R(b, u))). (Separation)
6. Let D be such a set. (Existence from 5)
7. C subseteq D. (Subset proof below)
 begin subproof
  7.1. Let u in C. (Assumption for subset proof)
  7.2. In(u, C) iff (In(u, X) and R(a, u)). (Universal Instantiation from 4)
  7.3. In(u, C) -> (In(u, X) and R(a, u)). (Biconditional Elimination from 7.2)
  7.4. In(u, X) and R(a, u). (Modus Ponens from 7.3, 7.1)
  7.5. R(a, u). (Conjunction Elimination from 7.4)
  7.6. R(b, a). (Relation Symmetry from 2)
  7.7. R(b, u). (Relation Transitivity from 7.6, 7.5)
  7.8. In(u, X). (Conjunction Elimination from 7.4)
  7.9. In(u, X) and R(b, u). (Conjunction Introduction from 7.8, 7.7)
  7.10. In(u, D) iff (In(u, X) and R(b, u)). (Universal Instantiation from 6)
  7.11. (In(u, X) and R(b, u)) -> In(u, D). (Biconditional Elimination from 7.10)
  7.12. In(u, D). (Modus Ponens from 7.11, 7.9)
 end subproof

# 4: Equal equivalence classes imply related representatives
## Proof that
### if the class of a equals the class of b then R(a, b)

1. Let X be any set, R be an equivalence relation on X, and a, b be in X. (Declaration)
2. exists C, forall u, (In(u, C) iff (In(u, X) and R(a, u))). (Separation)
3. Let C be such a set. (Existence from 2)
4. exists D, forall u, (In(u, D) iff (In(u, X) and R(b, u))). (Separation)
5. Let D be such a set. (Existence from 4)
6. C = D. (Premise)
7. R(a, a). (Relation Reflexivity from 1)
8. In(a, X) and R(a, a). (Conjunction Introduction from 1, 7)
9. In(a, C). (Set property from 3, 8)
10. In(a, D). (Substitution from 6, 9)
11. In(a, D) iff (In(a, X) and R(b, a)). (Universal Instantiation from 5)
12. In(a, X) and R(b, a). (Set property from 5, 10)
13. R(b, a). (Conjunction Elimination from 12)
14. R(a, b). (Relation Symmetry from 13)

# 5: Two equivalence classes with a common member are equal
## Proof that
### if a member belongs to the class of a and the class of b then the classes are equal

1. Let X be any set, R be an equivalence relation on X, and a, b, u be in X. (Declaration)
2. exists C, forall v, (In(v, C) iff (In(v, X) and R(a, v))). (Separation)
3. Let C be such a set. (Existence from 2)
4. exists D, forall v, (In(v, D) iff (In(v, X) and R(b, v))). (Separation)
5. Let D be such a set. (Existence from 4)
6. In(u, C). (Premise)
7. In(u, D). (Premise)
8. In(u, C) iff (In(u, X) and R(a, u)). (Universal Instantiation from 3)
9. In(u, X) and R(a, u). (Set property from 3, 6)
10. In(u, D) iff (In(u, X) and R(b, u)). (Universal Instantiation from 5)
11. In(u, X) and R(b, u). (Set property from 5, 7)
12. R(a, u). (Conjunction Elimination from 9)
13. R(b, u). (Conjunction Elimination from 11)
14. R(u, b). (Relation Symmetry from 13)
15. R(a, b). (Relation Transitivity from 12, 14)
16. C subseteq D. (Subset proof below)
  16.1. Let v in C. (Assumption for subset proof)
  16.2. In(v, C) iff (In(v, X) and R(a, v)). (Universal Instantiation from 3)
  16.3. In(v, C) -> (In(v, X) and R(a, v)). (Biconditional Elimination from 16.2)
  16.4. In(v, X) and R(a, v). (Modus Ponens from 16.3, 16.1)
  16.5. R(a, v). (Conjunction Elimination from 16.4)
  16.6. R(b, a). (Relation Symmetry from 15)
  16.7. R(b, v). (Relation Transitivity from 16.6, 16.5)
  16.8. In(v, X). (Conjunction Elimination from 16.4)
  16.9. In(v, X) and R(b, v). (Conjunction Introduction from 16.8, 16.7)
  16.10. In(v, D) iff (In(v, X) and R(b, v)). (Universal Instantiation from 5)
  16.11. (In(v, X) and R(b, v)) -> In(v, D). (Biconditional Elimination from 16.10)
  16.12. In(v, D). (Modus Ponens from 16.11, 16.9)
17. D subseteq C. (Subset proof below)
  17.1. Let v in D. (Assumption for subset proof)
  17.2. In(v, D) iff (In(v, X) and R(b, v)). (Universal Instantiation from 5)
  17.3. In(v, D) -> (In(v, X) and R(b, v)). (Biconditional Elimination from 17.2)
  17.4. In(v, X) and R(b, v). (Modus Ponens from 17.3, 17.1)
  17.5. R(b, v). (Conjunction Elimination from 17.4)
  17.6. R(a, b). (Reiteration from 15)
  17.7. R(a, v). (Relation Transitivity from 17.6, 17.5)
  17.8. In(v, X). (Conjunction Elimination from 17.4)
  17.9. In(v, X) and R(a, v). (Conjunction Introduction from 17.8, 17.7)
  17.10. In(v, C) iff (In(v, X) and R(a, v)). (Universal Instantiation from 3)
  17.11. (In(v, X) and R(a, v)) -> In(v, C). (Biconditional Elimination from 17.10)
  17.12. In(v, C). (Modus Ponens from 17.11, 17.9)
18. C = D. (Set Equality from 16, 17)

# 6: Every object in the carrier belongs to a named equivalence class

4. forall u, (In(u, C) iff (In(u, X) and R(a, u))). (Universal Generalization from subproof below)
begin subproof
 4.0. let c be arbitrary. (Arbitrary)
 4.1. In(c, C) iff (In(c, X) and R(a, c)). (Universal Instantiation from 3)
end subproof
5. R(a, a). (Relation Reflexivity from 1)
6. In(a, X) and R(a, a). (Conjunction Introduction from 1, 5)

9. exists C, ((forall u, (In(u, C) iff (In(u, X) and R(a, u))) and In(a, C)). (Existential Introduction from 8)

# 7: Symmetry cannot be used when the relation has not been declared symmetric
## Invalid: undeclared symmetry — the relation is reflexive and transitive, but not declared symmetric.
### then R(b, a).
