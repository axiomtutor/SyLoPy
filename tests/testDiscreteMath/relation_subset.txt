(*
A relation on X is a subset of X x X. `Let R be a relation on X` therefore also
states, as a fact of its line,

    forall x, forall y, (R(x, y) -> (In(x, X) and In(y, X)))

so a proof can get `In(a, X)` and `In(b, X)` from `R(a, b)` with the ordinary
rules, for terms declared in no set at all. The rules that start from
membership (reflexivity, irreflexivity, totality) can then be used on them.
Each invalid case has a valid twin that differs from it only in the condition
its title names.
*)

Use discrete math.

# 1: A related pair lies in the carrier
## Proof that
### if R is a relation on X and R(a, b), then a and b are in X,
### then In(a, X) and In(b, X).

1. Let X be any set, R be a relation on X, a, b be objects, R(a,b). (Declaration)
2. forall y, (R(a,y) -> (In(a,X) and In(y,X))). (Universal Instantiation from 1)
3. In(a,X) and In(b,X). (Universal Modus Ponens from 2, 1)

# 2: A reflexive relation relates every element of a related pair to itself
## Proof that
### if R is a reflexive relation on X and R(a, b), then R(a, a),
### then R(a, a).

1. Let X be any set, R be a reflexive relation on X, a, b be objects, R(a,b). (Declaration)
2. forall y, (R(a,y) -> (In(a,X) and In(y,X))). (Universal Instantiation from 1)
3. In(a,X) and In(b,X). (Universal Modus Ponens from 2, 1)
4. In(a,X). (Conjunction Elimination from 3)
5. R(a,a). (Relation Reflexivity from 4)

# 3: Membership in the carrier does not give reflexivity to a relation that is not declared reflexive
## Invalid: undeclared reflexivity — R is declared symmetric, and a relation on X is not thereby reflexive.
### then R(a, a).

1. Let X be any set, R be a symmetric relation on X, a, b be objects, R(a,b). (Declaration)
2. forall y, (R(a,y) -> (In(a,X) and In(y,X))). (Universal Instantiation from 1)
3. In(a,X) and In(b,X). (Universal Modus Ponens from 2, 1)
4. In(a,X). (Conjunction Elimination from 3)
5. R(a,a). (Relation Reflexivity from 4)

# 4: A relation on X says nothing of another set
## Invalid: other set — the declaration says that R relates members of X, not of Y.
### then In(a, Y) and In(b, Y).

1. Let X be any set, Y be any set, R be a relation on X, a, b be objects, R(a,b). (Declaration)
2. forall y, (R(a,y) -> (In(a,Y) and In(y,Y))). (Universal Instantiation from 1)
3. In(a,Y) and In(b,Y). (Universal Modus Ponens from 2, 1)

# 5: A relation on X puts only what it relates in X
## Invalid: unrelated element — R(a, b) puts a and b in X, and nothing relates c.
### then In(c, X) and In(b, X).

1. Let X be any set, R be a relation on X, a, b, c be objects, R(a,b). (Declaration)
2. forall y, (R(c,y) -> (In(c,X) and In(y,X))). (Universal Instantiation from 1)
3. In(c,X) and In(b,X). (Universal Modus Ponens from 2, 1)

# 6: A relation declared on a line after its carrier states the same
## Proof that
### if R is a relation on X, declared after X, and R(a, b), then a and b are in X,
### then In(a, X) and In(b, X).

1. Let X be any set. (Declaration)
2. Let R be a relation on X, a, b be objects, R(a,b). (Declaration)
3. forall y, (R(a,y) -> (In(a,X) and In(y,X))). (Universal Instantiation from 2)
4. In(a,X) and In(b,X). (Universal Modus Ponens from 3, 2)

# 7: A derived relation fact puts its terms in the carrier
## Proof that
### if R is a transitive relation on X, R(a, b) and R(b, c), then c is in X,
### then In(c, X).

1. Let X be any set, R be a transitive relation on X, a, b, c be objects, R(a,b) and R(b,c). (Declaration)
2. R(a,c). (Relation Transitivity from 1, 1)
3. forall y, (R(a,y) -> (In(a,X) and In(y,X))). (Universal Instantiation from 1)
4. In(a,X) and In(c,X). (Universal Modus Ponens from 3, 2)
5. In(c,X). (Conjunction Elimination from 4)

# 8: Each relation of a declaration states its own subset fact
## Proof that
### if R and S are relations on X and S(a, b), then a and b are in X,
### then In(a, X) and In(b, X).

1. Let X be any set, R and S be relations on X, a, b be objects, S(a,b). (Declaration)
2. forall y, (S(a,y) -> (In(a,X) and In(y,X))). (Universal Instantiation from 1)
3. In(a,X) and In(b,X). (Universal Modus Ponens from 2, 1)

# 9: The subset fact of one relation is repeated for another relation on the same carrier
## Proof that
### if R and S are relations on X, R(a, b) and S(a, b),
### then S(a, b) -> (In(a, X) and In(b, X)).

1. Let X be any set, R and S be relations on X, a, b be objects, R(a,b) and S(a,b). (Declaration)
2. R(a,b) -> (In(a,X) and In(b,X)). (Conditional Introduction from subproof below)
begin subproof
 2.1. R(a,b). (Assumption for Conditional Introduction)
 2.2. forall y, (R(a,y) -> (In(a,X) and In(y,X))). (Universal Instantiation from 1)
 2.3. In(a,X) and In(b,X). (Universal Modus Ponens from 2.2, 2.1)
end subproof
3. S(a,b) -> (In(a,X) and In(b,X)). (Mutatis mutandis, 2)

# 10: The subset fact of one relation is not repeated for a relation on another carrier
## Invalid: other carrier — line 1 says that R relates members of X, and nothing says that of S, which is declared on Y.
### then S(a, b) -> (In(a, X) and In(b, X)).

1. Let X be any set, Y be any set, R be a relation on X, S be a relation on Y, a, b be objects, R(a,b) and S(a,b). (Declaration)
2. R(a,b) -> (In(a,X) and In(b,X)). (Conditional Introduction from subproof below)

 2.2. forall y, (R(a,y) -> (In(a,X) and In(y,X))). (Universal Instantiation from 1)

end subproof
3. S(a,b) -> (In(a,X) and In(b,X)). (Mutatis mutandis, 2)
