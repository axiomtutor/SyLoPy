(*
Mutatis mutandis with relations: the argument for one relation, repeated for
another. Nothing says which rules of one relation correspond to which of the
other. The argument is written out for the other relation and validated, and a
rule that reads what a relation is declared to be (Relation Transitivity,
Relation Symmetry) reads the declaration of the other one at that line. So the
line is accepted when the new relation is declared to have the property the
rule needs and refused, by the rule, when it is not. The facts the argument
cites must be stated of the new relation too. Every invalid case has a valid
case that differs from it only in the point it names.
*)

Use discrete math.

# 1: A transitivity argument for one equivalence relation, repeated for another
## Proof that
### if R and S are equivalence relations on X, R(a,b), R(b,c), S(a,b) and S(b,c),
### then R(a, c) and S(a, c).

1. Let X be any set,
 R and S be equivalence relations on X,
 a, b, c be in X,
 R(a,b) and R(b,c) and S(a,b) and S(b,c). (Declaration)
2. R(a,c). (Relation Transitivity from 1, 1)
3. S(a,c). (Mutatis mutandis, 2)
4. R(a,c) and S(a,c). (Conjunction Introduction from 2, 3)

# 2: A symmetry argument for one symmetric relation, repeated for another
## Proof that
### if R and S are symmetric relations on X, R(a,b) and S(a,b),
### then R(b, a) and S(b, a).

1. Let X be any set,
 R and S be symmetric relations on X,
 a, b be in X,
 R(a,b) and S(a,b). (Declaration)
2. R(b,a). (Relation Symmetry from 1)
3. S(b,a). (Mutatis mutandis, 2)
4. R(b,a) and S(b,a). (Conjunction Introduction from 2, 3)

# 3: The new relation is not declared transitive
## Invalid: rule — Relation Transitivity does not justify S(a, c): S is declared symmetric, not transitive.
### then S(a, c).

1. Let X be any set,
 R be an equivalence relation on X,
 S be a symmetric relation on X,
 a, b, c be in X,
 R(a,b) and R(b,c) and S(a,b) and S(b,c). (Declaration)
2. R(a,c). (Relation Transitivity from 1, 1)
3. S(a,c). (Mutatis mutandis, 2)

# 4: The new relation is not declared symmetric
## Invalid: rule — Relation Symmetry does not justify S(b, a): S is declared reflexive, not symmetric.
### then S(b, a).

1. Let X be any set,
 R be a symmetric relation on X,
 S be a reflexive relation on X,
 a, b be in X,
 R(a,b) and S(a,b). (Declaration)
2. R(b,a). (Relation Symmetry from 1)
3. S(b,a). (Mutatis mutandis, 2)

# 5: The facts about the new relation are not stated
## Invalid: cited line — line 2 cites line 1, which says R(a, b) and R(b, c), and nothing says S(a, b) and S(b, c).
### then S(a, c).

1. Let X be any set,
 R and S be equivalence relations on X,
 a, b, c be in X,
 R(a,b) and R(b,c). (Declaration)
2. R(a,c). (Relation Transitivity from 1, 1)
3. S(a,c). (Mutatis mutandis, 2)

# 6: A transitivity formula does not make a relation declaration transitive
## Invalid: declaration property — Relation Transitivity reads the declaration of S, not a premise formula.
### then S(a, c).

1. Let X be any set,
 R be an equivalence relation on X,
 S be a relation on X,
 a, b, c be in X,
 R(a,b) and R(b,c) and S(a,b) and S(b,c). (Declaration)
2. forall x, forall y, forall z, ((S(x,y) and S(y,z)) -> S(x,z)). (Premise)
3. R(a,c). (Relation Transitivity from 1, 1)
4. S(a,c). (Mutatis mutandis, 3)

# 7: A declared transitive relation supports the repeated step
## Proof that
### if S is transitive on X and S(a,b) and S(b,c), then S(a,c).

1. Let X be any set,
 R be an equivalence relation on X,
 S be a transitive relation on X,
 a, b, c be in X,
 R(a,b) and R(b,c) and S(a,b) and S(b,c). (Declaration)
2. forall x, forall y, forall z, ((S(x,y) and S(y,z)) -> S(x,z)). (Premise)
3. R(a,c). (Relation Transitivity from 1, 1)
4. S(a,c). (Mutatis mutandis, 3)
