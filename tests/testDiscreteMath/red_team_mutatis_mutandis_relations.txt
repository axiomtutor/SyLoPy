(*
J26 red-team cases for declaration-dependent relation rules.
The invalid case has the relation property only as a formula premise; the
valid twin changes only the relation's declaration to include that property.
*)

Use discrete math.

# 1: A formula does not add transitivity to a relation declaration, and exchanging relation symbols fails if only one is transitive
## Invalid: declaration property — S is not declared transitive, and exchanging R and S fails despite the transitivity formula premise.
### then (S(a,b) and S(b,c)) -> (S(a,c) and R(a,b)).

1. Let X be any set,
 R be an equivalence relation on X,
 S be a symmetric relation on X,
 a, b, c be in X,
 R(a,b) and R(b,c) and S(a,b) and S(b,c). (Declaration)
2. forall x, forall y, forall z, ((S(x,y) and S(y,z)) -> S(x,z)). (Premise)
3. (R(a,b) and R(b,c)) -> (R(a,c) and S(a,b)). (Conditional Introduction from subproof below)
begin subproof
 3.1. R(a,b) and R(b,c). (Assumption for Conditional Introduction)
 3.2. R(a,b). (Conjunction Elimination from 3.1)
 3.3. R(b,c). (Conjunction Elimination from 3.1)
 3.4. R(a,c). (Relation Transitivity from 3.2, 3.3)
 3.5. S(a,b). (Reiteration from 1)
 3.6. R(a,c) and S(a,b). (Conjunction Introduction from 3.4, 3.5)
end subproof
4. (S(a,b) and S(b,c)) -> (S(a,c) and R(a,b)). (Mutatis mutandis, 3)

# 2: Exchanging relation symbols works when both have the required property
## Proof that
### if R(a,b), R(b,c), S(a,b) and S(b,c),
### then (S(a,b) and S(b,c)) -> (S(a,c) and R(a,b)).

1. Let X be any set,
 R be an equivalence relation on X,
 S be a transitive relation on X,
 a, b, c be in X,
 R(a,b) and R(b,c) and S(a,b) and S(b,c). (Declaration)
2. forall x, forall y, forall z, ((S(x,y) and S(y,z)) -> S(x,z)). (Premise)
3. (R(a,b) and R(b,c)) -> (R(a,c) and S(a,b)). (Conditional Introduction from subproof below)
begin subproof
 3.1. R(a,b) and R(b,c). (Assumption for Conditional Introduction)
 3.2. R(a,b). (Conjunction Elimination from 3.1)
 3.3. R(b,c). (Conjunction Elimination from 3.1)
 3.4. R(a,c). (Relation Transitivity from 3.2, 3.3)
 3.5. S(a,b). (Reiteration from 1)
 3.6. R(a,c) and S(a,b). (Conjunction Introduction from 3.4, 3.5)
end subproof
4. (S(a,b) and S(b,c)) -> (S(a,c) and R(a,b)). (Mutatis mutandis, 3)

# 3: A relation declared reflexive on another carrier cannot discharge membership in X
## Invalid: carrier mismatch — S is declared reflexive on Y, but only membership in X is established.
### then S(a, a).

1. Let X and Y be any sets,
 R be a reflexive relation on X,
 S be a reflexive relation on Y,
 a be in X. (Declaration)
2. a is in X. (Premise)
3. R(a, a). (Relation Reflexivity from 2)
4. S(a, a). (Mutatis mutandis, 3)

# 4: A relation declared reflexive on X supports the repeated step
## Proof that
### if a is in X,
### then S(a, a).

1. Let X and Y be any sets,
 R be a reflexive relation on X,
 S be a reflexive relation on X,
 a be in X. (Declaration)
2. a is in X. (Premise)
3. R(a, a). (Relation Reflexivity from 2)
4. S(a, a). (Mutatis mutandis, 3)
