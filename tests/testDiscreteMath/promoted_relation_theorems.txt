Use discrete math.

(*
A theorem proved about the relation of a declaration can be cited for another
relation. What it needs of the citation:

  - its premises, said about the relation it is cited for (a premise about the
    theorem's own relation is no premise about the other one);
  - a declaration of that relation, visible at the citation, with the carrier
    and the properties that the theorem's proof read from its own declaration
    (the relation rules read them, and no premise states them). The carrier is
    a name: a compound term such as `A union B` is not what a declaration says.

Each invalid proof has a valid twin in this file that differs by the one thing
the invalid proof lacks, so the refusal cannot come from parsing, declarations
or scope.
*)

# 1: Symmetric relations reverse every pair
## Proof that
### a symmetric relation reverses every pair
1. Let X be any set, R be a symmetric relation on X. (Declaration)
2. forall a, forall b, (R(a,b) -> R(b,a)). (Universal Generalization from subproof below)
begin subproof
 2.0. let a be arbitrary. (Fresh Variable)
 2.1. forall b, (R(a,b) -> R(b,a)). (Universal Generalization from subproof below)
 begin subproof
  2.1.0. let b be arbitrary. (Fresh Variable)
  2.1.1. R(a,b) -> R(b,a). (Conditional Introduction from subproof below)
  begin subproof
   2.1.1.1. R(a,b). (Assumption for Conditional Introduction)
   2.1.1.2. R(b,a). (Relation Symmetry from 2.1.1.1)
  end subproof
 end subproof
end subproof

# 2: Reflexive relations relate each element of the carrier to itself
## Proof that
### a is in X and R(a,a)
1. Let X be any set, R be a reflexive relation on X, a be in X. (Declaration)
2. R(a,a). (Relation Reflexivity from 1)
3. In(a,X) and R(a,a). (Conjunction Introduction from 1, 2)

# 3: Symmetry from a premise
## Proof that
### R(u,v) -> R(v,u) from a symmetry premise
1. Let X be any set, R be a relation on X. (Declaration)
2. forall a, forall b, (R(a,b) -> R(b,a)). (Premise)
3. Let u, v be in X. (Declaration)
4. forall b, (R(u,b) -> R(b,u)). (Universal Instantiation from 2)
5. R(u,v) -> R(v,u). (Universal Instantiation from 4)

# 6

# 7
## Proof that
### premises about the relation the theorem is cited for give its conclusion
1. Let X be any set, S be a relation on X. (Declaration)
2. forall a, forall b, (S(a,b) -> S(b,a)). (Premise)
3. Let u, v be in X. (Declaration)
4. S(u,v) -> S(v,u). (Symmetry from a premise from 1, 2, 3)

# 8
## Invalid proof that
### premises about the theorem's own relation do not give its conclusion for another relation
1. Let X be any set, R be a relation on X. (Declaration)

3. Let S be a relation on X. (Declaration)
4. Let u, v be in X. (Declaration)
5. S(u,v) -> S(v,u). (Symmetry from a premise from 1, 2, 4)

# 10
## Invalid proof that
### a relation reflexive on X is not reflexive on A union B
1. Let A, B, X be any sets, R be a reflexive relation on X, a be an object. (Declaration)
2. forall x, forall y, (R(x,y) -> (In(x, A union B) and In(y, A union B))). (Premise)
3. In(a, A union B). (Premise)
4. In(a, A union B) and R(a,a). (Reflexive relations relate each element of the carrier to itself from 2, 3)
