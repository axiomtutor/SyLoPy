(*
J26 red-team cases for term substitutions in Set property.
The invalid case lacks a visible membership characterization after replacing
a by f(a); its valid twin adds precisely that counterpart.
*)

# 1: A term replacement needs a transformed membership characterization
## Invalid: missing counterpart — no visible line characterizes membership in Y by equality to f(a).
### then In(f(a), Y).

1. Let a and b be any sets and f be a function and Y be any set. (Declaration)
2. forall u, (In(u, Y) iff u = a). (Premise)
3. forall u, (In(u, Y) iff u = b). (Premise)
4. a = a. (Reflexivity)
5. f(a) = f(a). (Reflexivity)
6. In(a, Y). (Set property from 2, 4)
7. In(f(a), Y). (Mutatis mutandis, 6)

# 2: A term replacement is valid when its membership characterization is stated
## Proof that
### if membership in Y is characterized by equality to a and f(a),
### then In(f(a), Y).

1. Let a and b be any sets and f be a function and Y be any set. (Declaration)
2. forall u, (In(u, Y) iff u = a). (Premise)
3. forall u, (In(u, Y) iff u = f(a)). (Premise)
4. a = a. (Reflexivity)
5. f(a) = f(a). (Reflexivity)
6. In(a, Y). (Set property from 2, 4)
7. In(f(a), Y). (Mutatis mutandis, 6)

# 3: A set name may be replaced by a term only when the characterization is available
## Invalid: missing counterpart — no visible line characterizes membership in f(Y).
### then In(a, f(Y)).

1. Let a be any set, Y and Z be any sets, and f be a function. (Declaration)
2. forall u, (In(u, Y) iff u = a). (Premise)
3. forall u, (In(u, Z) iff u = a). (Premise)
4. a = a. (Reflexivity)
5. In(a, Y). (Set property from 2, 4)
6. In(a, f(Y)). (Mutatis mutandis, 5)

# 4: A set name may be replaced by a term when its characterization is stated
## Proof that
### if membership in Y and f(Y) is characterized by equality to a,
### then a is in f(Y).

1. Let a be any set, Y and Z be any sets, and f be a function. (Declaration)
2. forall u, (In(u, Y) iff u = a). (Premise)
3. forall u, (In(u, f(Y)) iff u = a). (Premise)
4. a = a. (Reflexivity)
5. In(a, Y). (Set property from 2, 4)
6. In(a, f(Y)). (Mutatis mutandis, 5)
