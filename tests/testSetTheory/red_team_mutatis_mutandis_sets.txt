(*
J26 red-team cases for membership-specific SetProperty and axiom-citation rules.
Each refusal has a valid control that changes only whether In is replaced.
*)

# 1: Set property cannot replace membership by an arbitrary predicate
## Invalid: membership symbol — SetProperty requires In even when the matching R-characterization is visible.
### then R(a, f(Y)).

1. Let a and Y be any sets, f be a function and R be a predicate. (Declaration)
2. forall u, (In(u, Y) iff u = a). (Premise)
3. forall u, (R(u, f(Y)) iff u = a). (Premise)
4. a = a. (Reflexivity)
5. In(a, Y). (Set property from 2, 4)
6. R(a, f(Y)). (Mutatis mutandis, 5)

# 2: Set property supports a set-name-to-term replacement when its characterization is present
## Proof that
### if membership in Y and f(Y) is characterized by equality to a,
### then In(a, f(Y)).

1. Let a and Y be any sets, f be a function and R be a predicate. (Declaration)
2. forall u, (In(u, Y) iff u = a). (Premise)
3. forall u, (In(u, f(Y)) iff u = a). (Premise)
4. a = a. (Reflexivity)
5. In(a, Y). (Set property from 2, 4)
6. In(a, f(Y)). (Mutatis mutandis, 5)

# 3: Pairing cannot replace its membership symbol by a predicate
## Invalid: axiom-specific symbol — the Pairing axiom requires In, not R.
### then exists Y, forall u, (R(u, Y) iff (u = f(a) or u = b)).

1. Let a and b be any sets and f be a function and R be a predicate. (Declaration)
2. exists Y, forall u, (In(u, Y) iff (u = a or u = b)). (Axiom of pairing)
3. exists Y, forall u, (R(u, Y) iff (u = f(a) or u = b)). (Mutatis mutandis, 2)

# 4: Pairing permits a name-to-term replacement while keeping membership fixed
## Proof that
### if a and b are sets and f is a function,
### then exists Y, forall u, (In(u, Y) iff (u = f(a) or u = b)).

1. Let a and b be any sets and f be a function and R be a predicate. (Declaration)
2. exists Y, forall u, (In(u, Y) iff (u = a or u = b)). (Axiom of pairing)
3. exists Y, forall u, (In(u, Y) iff (u = f(a) or u = b)). (Mutatis mutandis, 2)
