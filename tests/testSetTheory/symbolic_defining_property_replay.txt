(
Symbolic defining-property replay in nested subproofs. Cases 1 and 3 are
positive; cases 2 and 4 keep the Mutatis and WLOG replay guards strict.
)

# 1: Symbolic declaration cited outside a repeated subproof
## Proof that
### a symbolic set property can be replayed by Mutatis mutandis inside a subproof.

1. Let P be a unary predicate. (Declaration)
2. Let a, b be objects. (Declaration)
3. Let Y be a set with defining property P. (Declaration)
4. In(a, Y). (Premise)
5. In(b, Y). (Premise)
6. P(a) -> P(a). (Conditional Introduction from subproof below)
begin subproof
 6.1. P(a). (Assumption for Conditional Introduction)
 6.2. P(a). (Set property from 3, 4)
end subproof
7. P(b) -> P(b). (Mutatis mutandis, 6)

# 2: Mutatis must not repeat a declaration added inside its argument
## Invalid: the nested declaration is new structure in the argument.

1. Let P be a unary predicate. (Declaration)
2. Let a, b be objects. (Declaration)
3. P(a) -> P(a). (Conditional Introduction from subproof below)
begin subproof
 3.1. P(a). (Assumption for Conditional Introduction)
 3.2. Let Y be a set with defining property P. (Declaration)
 3.3. P(a). (Reiteration from 3.1)
end subproof
4. P(b) -> P(b). (Mutatis mutandis, 3)

# 3: WLOG replay with an exact symbolic declaration in a nested subproof
## Proof that
### a nested symbolic declaration and its citation replay to the other WLOG case.

1. Let a, b be objects. (Declaration)
2. Let P be a unary predicate. (Declaration)
3. Let H, Q be closed formulas. (Declaration)
4. H -> Q. (Premise)
5. P(a). (Premise)
6. P(b). (Premise)
7. P(a) or P(b). (Premise)
8. P(a). (WLOG from 7)
9. H -> Q. (Conditional Introduction from subproof below)
begin subproof
 9.1. H. (Assumption for Conditional Introduction)
 9.2. Let Y be a set with defining property P. (Declaration)
 9.3. P(a). (Reiteration from 5)
 9.4. Q. (Modus Ponens from 9.1, 4)
end subproof

# 4: WLOG replay rejects a missing counterpart in the nested case
## Invalid: P(b) has no visible counterpart for the replay of line 5.

1. Let a, b be objects. (Declaration)
2. Let P be a unary predicate. (Declaration)
3. Let H, Q be closed formulas. (Declaration)
4. H -> Q. (Premise)
5. P(a). (Premise)
6. P(a) or P(b). (Premise)
7. P(a). (WLOG from 6)
8. H -> Q. (Conditional Introduction from subproof below)
begin subproof
 8.1. H. (Assumption for Conditional Introduction)
 8.2. Let Y be a set with defining property P. (Declaration)
 8.3. P(a). (Reiteration from 5)
 8.4. Q. (Modus Ponens from 8.1, 4)
end subproof
