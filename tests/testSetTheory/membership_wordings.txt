# J37: every membership wording denotes the membership atom; definitions give operation laws.

# 1: A is a subset of a union containing it
## Proof that
### then forall u, (In(u, A) -> In(u, Union(A, B))).
1. Let A, B be any set. (Declaration)
2. forall u, (In(u, Union(A, B)) iff (In(u, A) or In(u, B))). (Definition of union)
3. A is a subset of the union of A and B. (Subset proof below)
 3.1. Let x be in A. (Assumption for subset proof)
 3.2. In(x, A) or In(x, B). (Disjunction Introduction from 3.1)
 3.3. x is in the union of A and B. (Set property from 2, 3.2)

# 2: Membership in the union leads to a case split
## Proof that
### then Q(x).
1. Let A, B be any set, and x be any object. (Declaration)
2. Let Q be a predicate. (Declaration)
3. x is in the union of A and B. (Premise)
4. In(x, A) -> Q(x). (Premise)
5. In(x, B) -> Q(x). (Premise)
6. forall u, (In(u, Union(A, B)) iff (In(u, A) or In(u, B))). (Definition of union)
7. In(x, A) or In(x, B). (Set property from 6, 3)
8. Q(x). (Proof by Cases from 7, subproofs below)
 begin subproof
  8.1. In(x, A). (Case)
  8.2. Q(x). (Modus Ponens from 4, 8.1)
 end subproof
 begin subproof
  8.1. In(x, B). (Case)
  8.2. Q(x). (Modus Ponens from 5, 8.1)
 end subproof

# 3: Invalid: membership in a union does not expose the disjunction by itself
## Invalid: the definition of union is needed to derive either disjunctive membership fact.
### then In(x, A) or In(x, B).
1. Let A, B be any set, and x be any object. (Declaration)
2. x is in the union of A and B. (Premise)
3. In(x, A) or In(x, B). (Set property from 2)

# 4: Membership in the intersection gives both members, by its definition
## Proof that
### then In(x, A).
1. Let A, B be any set, and x be any object. (Declaration)
2. x is in the intersection of A and B. (Premise)
3. forall u, (In(u, Intersection(A, B)) iff (In(u, A) and In(u, B))). (Definition of intersection)
4. In(x, A) and In(x, B). (Set property from 3, 2)
5. In(x, A). (Conjunction Elimination from 4)

# 5: Invalid: membership in an intersection does not expose a conjunct by itself

2. x is in the intersection of A and B. (Premise)
