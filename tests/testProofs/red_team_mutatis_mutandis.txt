(*
Independent red-team tests for Mutatis mutandis stage 1.
The existing mutatis_mutandis.txt covers the original 30 cases; these focus on
uncovered formula shapes, replacement patterns, case-block hypotheses, and
reusing one source line for multiple targets.

Stages 2 and 3 let a name be replaced by a term and a predicate or function
symbol by another. Cases 4, 17 and 20 asked for a refusal that stage 1 gave
because such a replacement was not allowed; they are still refused, now because
the facts the repeated argument needs about the new term or symbols are not
stated, and cases 21 to 23 are their valid twins.
*)

# 1: Equality sides can be exchanged when the counterpart is available
## Proof that
### if a and b are objects such that: a = b. b = a.
### then b = a.

1. Let a and b be objects such that: a = b. b = a. (Premise)
2. a = b. (Reiteration from 1)
3. b = a. (Mutatis mutandis, 2)

# 7: Repeating a proof by cases transforms both case blocks

2. P(a) or Q(a). (Reiteration from 1)
3. P(a) or Q(a). (Proof by Cases from 2, subproofs below)
begin subproof
 3.1.l. P(a). (Case)
 3.2.l. P(a) or Q(a). (Disjunction Introduction from 3.1.l)
end subproof
begin subproof
 3.1.r. Q(a). (Case)
 3.2.r. P(a) or Q(a). (Disjunction Introduction from 3.1.r)
end subproof
4. P(b) or Q(b). (Mutatis mutandis, 3)

# 13: A three-name rotation is a simultaneous replacement
## Proof that
### if a, b and c are objects and R is a predicate such that: forall x, forall y, forall z, R(x, y, z).
### then R(b, c, a).

1. Let a, b and c be objects and R be a predicate such that: forall x, forall y, forall z, R(x, y, z). (Premise)
2. forall y, forall z, R(a, y, z). (Universal Instantiation from 1)
3. forall z, R(a, b, z). (Universal Instantiation from 2)
4. R(a, b, c). (Universal Instantiation from 3)
5. forall y, forall z, R(b, y, z). (Universal Instantiation from 1)
6. forall z, R(b, c, z). (Universal Instantiation from 5)
7. R(b, c, a). (Mutatis mutandis, 4)
