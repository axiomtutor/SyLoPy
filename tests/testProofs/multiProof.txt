## Proof that
### if A, B, C are closed formulas such that: A -> B. B -> C. C -> A.
### then A -> C.
(* Proof with unnecessary steps. *)

1. Let A, B, C be closed formulas such that: A -> B. B -> C. C -> A. (Premise)
2. A -> C. (Hypothetical Syllogism from 1)
3. A <-> C. (Biconditional Introduction from 1,2)
4. C -> A. (Biconditional Elimination from 3)
5. A -> C. (Biconditional Elimination from 3)

# 2
## Proof that
### if A, B are closed formulas such that: not (if A then B).
### then A and not B.

1. Let A, B be closed formulas such that: not (if A then B). (Premise)
(* Comments can even come in the middle of a proof. *)
2. A and not B. (Proof by Contradiction from subproof below)
begin subproof
 2.1. not (A and not B). (Assumption for Contradiction)
 2.2. not A or not (not B). (De Morgans from 2.1)
 2.3. not A or B. (Double Negation from 2.2)
 2.4. if A then B. (Conditional Equivalence from 2.3)
 2.5. (if A then B) and not (if A then B). (Conjunction Introduction from 2.4, 1)
end subproof

# 3
## Proof that
### if A, B are closed formulas.
### then A -> (B -> A).
(* Doubly nested subproofs. *)

1. Let A, B be closed formulas. (Premise)
2. A -> (B -> A). (Conditional Introduction from subproof below)
begin subproof
 1.1. A. (Assumption for conditional introduction)
 1.2. B -> A. (Conditional Introduction from subproof below)
 begin subproof
  1.2.1. B. (Assumption for conditional introduction)
  1.2.2. A. (Reiteration from 1.1)
 end subproof
end subproof

# 4
## Proof that
### if P, Q, R are closed formulas such that: P or Q. if P then R. Q -> R.
### then R.

1. Let P, Q, R be closed formulas such that: P or Q. if P then R. Q -> R. (Premise)
2. R. (Proof by Cases from 1, subproofs below)
begin subproof
 2.1.case1. P. (Case)
 2.2.case1. R. (Modus Ponens from 1, 2.1.case1)
end subproof
begin subproof
 2.1.case2. Q. (Case)
 2.2.case2. R. (Modus Ponens from 1, 2.1.case2)
end subproof

# 6
## Proof that
### if a, b are objects and P is a predicate such that: forall x, forall y, P(f(x), g(y)).
### then exists w, exists z, P(w, z).

1. Let a, b be objects. (Premise)
2. Let P be a predicate such that: forall x, forall y, P(f(x), g(y)). (Premise)
3. forall y, P(f(a), g(y)). (Universal Instantiation from 2)
4. P(f(a), g(b)). (Universal Instantiation from 3)
5. exists z, P(f(a), z). (Existential Introduction from 4)
6. exists w, exists z, P(w, z). (Existential Introduction from 5)

# 7
## Proof that
### if A, B are closed formulas such that: A -> B. not B.
### then not A.

1. Let A, B be closed formulas such that: A -> B. not B. (Premise)
2. not A. (Modus Tollens from 1)
