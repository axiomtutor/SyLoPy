# Direct consequences of union and intersection membership properties.

# 1: Every member of A belongs to a set containing the union of A and B
## Proof that
### If forall u, (In(u, U) iff (In(u, A) or In(u, B)))
### then A is a subset of U.

1. Let A, B, U be any set. (Declaration)
2. forall u, (In(u, U) iff (In(u, A) or In(u, B))). (Premise)
3. A is a subset of U. (Subset proof below)
 3.1. Let x in A. (Assumption for subset proof)
 3.2. In(x, A) or In(x, B). (Disjunction Introduction from 3.1)
 3.3. In(x, U). (Set property from 2, 3.2)

# 3: Every member of an intersection belongs to A
## Proof that
### If forall u, (In(u, I) iff (In(u, A) and In(u, B)))

# 5: If A is a subset of B, their intersection is A
## Proof that
### If A is a subset of B, and forall u, (In(u, I) iff (In(u, A) and In(u, B)))
### then I = A.

1. Let A, B, I be any set. (Declaration)
2. A is a subset of B. (Premise)
3. forall u, (In(u, I) iff (In(u, A) and In(u, B))). (Premise)
4. I is a subset of A. (Subset proof below)
 4.1. Let x in I. (Assumption for subset proof)
 4.2. In(x, A) and In(x, B). (Set property from 3, 4.1)
 4.3. In(x, A). (Conjunction Elimination from 4.2)
5. A is a subset of I. (Subset proof below)
 5.1. Let y in A. (Assumption for subset proof)
 5.2. In(y, A) -> In(y, B). (Universal Instantiation from 2)
 5.3. In(y, B). (Modus Ponens from 5.2, 5.1)
 5.4. In(y, A) and In(y, B). (Conjunction Introduction from 5.1, 5.3)
 5.5. In(y, I). (Set property from 3, 5.4)
6. I = A. (Set Equality from 4, 5)
