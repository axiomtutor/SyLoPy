# 1: A product with a sum of integers is an integer
## Proof that
### Int(a), Int(b), and Int(c) imply Int(Times(Neg(a), Plus(b,c)))

1. Let a, b, c be objects. (Declaration)
2. Int(a). (Premise)
3. Int(b). (Premise)
4. Int(c). (Premise)
5. forall x, forall y, ((Int(x) and Int(y)) -> Int(Plus(x, y))). (Axiom)
6. forall y, ((Int(b) and Int(y)) -> Int(Plus(b, y))). (Universal Instantiation from 5)
7. (Int(b) and Int(c)) -> Int(Plus(b, c)). (Universal Instantiation from 6)
8. Int(b) and Int(c). (Conjunction Introduction from 3, 4)
9. Int(Plus(b, c)). (Modus Ponens from 7, 8)
10. forall x, (Int(x) -> Int(Neg(x))). (Axiom)
11. Int(a) -> Int(Neg(a)). (Universal Instantiation from 10)
12. Int(Neg(a)). (Modus Ponens from 11, 2)
13. forall x, forall y, ((Int(x) and Int(y)) -> Int(Times(x, y))). (Axiom)
14. forall y, ((Int(Neg(a)) and Int(y)) -> Int(Times(Neg(a), y))). (Universal Instantiation from 13)
15. (Int(Neg(a)) and Int(Plus(b, c))) -> Int(Times(Neg(a), Plus(b, c))). (Universal Instantiation from 14)
16. Int(Neg(a)) and Int(Plus(b, c)). (Conjunction Introduction from 12, 9)
17. Int(Times(Neg(a), Plus(b, c))). (Modus Ponens from 15, 16)
