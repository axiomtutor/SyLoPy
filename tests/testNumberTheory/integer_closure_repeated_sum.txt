# 1: Repeated sums of integer expressions preserve integerhood
## Proof that
### Int(a) and Int(b) imply Int(Plus(Plus(Neg(a), b), b))

1. Let a, b be objects. (Declaration)
2. Int(a). (Premise)
3. Int(b). (Premise)
4. forall x, (Int(x) -> Int(Neg(x))). (Axiom)
5. Int(a) -> Int(Neg(a)). (Universal Instantiation from 4)
6. Int(Neg(a)). (Modus Ponens from 5, 2)
7. forall x, forall y, ((Int(x) and Int(y)) -> Int(Plus(x, y))). (Axiom)
8. forall y, ((Int(Neg(a)) and Int(y)) -> Int(Plus(Neg(a), y))). (Universal Instantiation from 7)
9. (Int(Neg(a)) and Int(b)) -> Int(Plus(Neg(a), b)). (Universal Instantiation from 8)
10. Int(Neg(a)) and Int(b). (Conjunction Introduction from 6, 3)
11. Int(Plus(Neg(a), b)). (Modus Ponens from 9, 10)
12. forall y, ((Int(Plus(Neg(a), b)) and Int(y)) -> Int(Plus(Plus(Neg(a), b), y))). (Universal Instantiation from 7)
13. (Int(Plus(Neg(a), b)) and Int(b)) -> Int(Plus(Plus(Neg(a), b), b)). (Universal Instantiation from 12)
14. Int(Plus(Neg(a), b)) and Int(b). (Conjunction Introduction from 11, 3)
15. Int(Plus(Plus(Neg(a), b), b)). (Modus Ponens from 13, 14)
