# 1: A product of two integers is an integer, by the argument for the sum
## Proof that
### Int(a) and Int(b) imply Int(Times(a, b))

1. Let a, b be objects. (Declaration)
2. Int(a). (Premise)
3. Int(b). (Premise)
4. forall x, forall y, ((Int(x) and Int(y)) -> Int(Plus(x, y))). (Axiom)
5. forall x, forall y, ((Int(x) and Int(y)) -> Int(Times(x, y))). (Axiom)
6. (Int(a) and Int(b)) -> Int(Plus(a, b)). (Conditional Introduction from subproof below)
begin subproof
 6.1. Int(a) and Int(b). (Assumption for Conditional Introduction)
 6.2. forall y, ((Int(a) and Int(y)) -> Int(Plus(a, y))). (Universal Instantiation from 4)
 6.3. (Int(a) and Int(b)) -> Int(Plus(a, b)). (Universal Instantiation from 6.2)
 6.4. Int(Plus(a, b)). (Modus Ponens from 6.3, 6.1)
end subproof
7. (Int(a) and Int(b)) -> Int(Times(a, b)). (Mutatis mutandis, 6)
8. Int(a) and Int(b). (Conjunction Introduction from 2, 3)
9. Int(Times(a, b)). (Modus Ponens from 7, 8)
