# Intended readings for union, intersection, and disjointness phrasing.

id: j4-union-membership
context: line
surface: x is in the union of A and B
expect: In(x, A) or In(x, B)
why: Membership in a union means membership in at least one of its two sets.

id: j4-union-membership-nested
context: nested
surface: P(x) and x belongs to A union B
expect: P(x) and (In(x, A) or In(x, B))
why: The union-membership claim is the second conjunct.

id: j4-intersection-membership
context: line
surface: x belongs to the intersection of A and B
expect: In(x, A) and In(x, B)
why: Membership in an intersection means membership in both sets.

id: j4-intersection-membership-negated
context: nested
surface: x is not in the intersection of A and B
expect: not (In(x, A) and In(x, B))
why: Negating intersection membership negates the conjunction.

id: j4-union-defines-set
context: line
surface: A is the union of B and C
expect: forall u, (In(u, A) iff (In(u, B) or In(u, C)))
why: The union set contains exactly the elements belonging to B or C.

id: j4-intersection-defines-set
context: line
surface: A is the intersection of B and C
expect: forall u, (In(u, A) iff (In(u, B) and In(u, C)))
why: The intersection set contains exactly the elements belonging to both B and C.

id: j4-union-nested-consequence
context: nested
surface: if x is in the union of A and B then P(x)
expect: (In(x, A) or In(x, B)) -> P(x)
why: The union-membership condition is the antecedent of the implication.

id: j4-disjoint-adjective
context: line
surface: A and B are disjoint
expect: forall u, not (In(u, A) and In(u, B))
why: Disjoint sets have no common member.

id: j4-disjoint-from
context: nested
surface: A is disjoint from B and P(A)
expect: (forall u, not (In(u, A) and In(u, B))) and P(A)
why: Disjointness states the absence of any common member.

id: j4-not-disjoint
context: line
surface: A and B are not disjoint
expect: exists u, (In(u, A) and In(u, B))
why: Two sets are not disjoint exactly when they share a member.

id: j4-union-empty
context: nested
surface: the union of A and B is empty
expect: forall u, not (In(u, A) or In(u, B))
why: A union that is empty has no element from either set.

id: j4-intersection-nonempty
context: nested
surface: the intersection of A and B is nonempty
expect: exists u, (In(u, A) and In(u, B))
why: A common element witnesses a nonempty intersection.

id: definition-term-union-membership
context: nested
surface: P(x) and x is in A union B
expect: P(x) and In(x, Union(A, B))
why: "A union B" is a term, and "is in" asks whether x is a member of it. This differs from "x belongs to A union B" (j4-union-membership-nested), which is already spelled out as a disjunction. The sentence (Definition of union) cites relates the two.

id: definition-term-intersection-membership
context: nested
surface: P(x) and x is in A intersection B
expect: P(x) and In(x, Intersection(A, B))
why: Like the union: the term "A intersection B" is a member-of target, not spelled out as a conjunction. (Definition of intersection) relates the two.

id: definition-term-power-set-membership
context: nested
surface: P(x) and x is in the power set of A
expect: P(x) and In(x, PowerSet(A))
why: "the power set of A" is a term. (Definition of power set) says that its members are the subsets of A.

id: definition-term-product-membership
context: nested
surface: P(p) and p is in A cross B
expect: P(p) and In(p, CartesianProduct(A, B))
why: "A cross B" is the Cartesian product term. (Definition of Cartesian product) says that its members are the pairs of a member of A and a member of B.

id: definition-term-ordered-pair-equality
context: nested
surface: P(a) and pair(a, b) = pair(c, d)
expect: P(a) and Pair(a, b) = Pair(c, d)
why: "pair(a, b)" is the ordered pair term, so the sentence is an equation of two terms. (Definition of ordered pair) says when such an equation holds.

id: definition-bounded-product-clause
context: nested
surface: P(p) and exists a in A, exists b in B, p = pair(a, b)
expect: P(p) and exists a, (In(a, A) and exists b, (In(b, B) and p = Pair(a, b)))
why: The two bounded quantifiers are the clause of the definition of the Cartesian product, with each restriction as the first conjunct of its quantifier.
