# J41: mathematical symbols are notation for existing word forms.
# The expected side uses only the ordinary logical/set-theory vocabulary.

id: unicode-and
context: nested
surface: P ∧ Q
expect: P and Q
why: Conjunction's logical symbol has the same reading as the word connective.

id: unicode-or
context: line
surface: P ∨ Q
expect: P or Q
why: Disjunction's logical symbol has the same reading as the word connective.

id: unicode-negation
context: nested
surface: ¬P
expect: not P
why: Negation's symbol has the same reading as the word form.

id: unicode-implication
context: nested
surface: P → Q
expect: P -> Q
why: The right arrow denotes implication.

id: unicode-biconditional-existing
context: line
surface: P ↔ Q
expect: P iff Q
why: The already-supported biconditional symbol retains its existing reading.

id: unicode-forall-comma
context: nested
surface: ∀x, P(x)
expect: forall x, P(x)
why: The universal quantifier symbol introduces an ordinary universal quantifier.

id: unicode-forall-no-comma
context: line
surface: ∀x P(x)
expect: forall x, P(x)
why: The universal quantifier symbol supports the existing optional-comma form.

id: unicode-exists
context: nested
surface: ∃x, P(x)
expect: exists x, P(x)
why: The existential quantifier symbol introduces an ordinary existential quantifier.

id: unicode-bounded-forall
context: nested
surface: ∀x ∈ A, P(x)
expect: forall x, (In(x, A) -> P(x))
why: Membership restricts the range of the universal quantifier.

id: unicode-bounded-exists
context: line
surface: ∃x ∈ A, P(x)
expect: exists x, (In(x, A) and P(x))
why: Membership restricts the witness of the existential quantifier.

id: unicode-membership
context: line
surface: x ∈ A
expect: In(x, A)
why: The membership symbol denotes the set-theory membership predicate.

id: unicode-nonmembership
context: nested
surface: x ∉ A
expect: not (In(x, A))
why: Nonmembership denotes the negation of the membership predicate.

id: unicode-subseteq
context: line
surface: A ⊆ B
expect: forall q, (In(q, A) -> In(q, B))
why: Subset inclusion is the universal implication between memberships.

id: unicode-union
context: nested
surface: x ∈ A ∪ B
expect: In(x, Union(A, B))
why: The union symbol is the binary set-operation term in a membership formula.

id: unicode-intersection
context: nested
surface: x ∈ A ∩ B
expect: In(x, Intersection(A, B))
why: The intersection symbol is the binary set-operation term in a membership formula.

id: unicode-cartesian-product
context: line
surface: x ∈ A × B
expect: In(x, CartesianProduct(A, B))
why: The multiplication-shaped symbol denotes Cartesian product in set theory.

id: unicode-not-equal
context: nested
surface: a ≠ b
expect: not (a = b)
why: The not-equal symbol is negated equality.

id: unicode-not-equal-ascii
context: line
surface: a != b
expect: not (a = b)
why: The ASCII not-equal spelling has the same reading as the Unicode symbol.

id: unicode-no-spaces-membership
context: nested
surface: x∈A
expect: In(x, A)
why: Whitespace is not required around a notation symbol.

id: unicode-no-spaces-connectives
context: nested
surface: ¬P(x)∧Q(x)
expect: (not P(x)) and Q(x)
why: Adjacent symbols are padded before parsing and preserve connective precedence.

id: unicode-symbol-adjacent-to-word
context: nested
surface: x ∈ A and P(x)
expect: In(x, A) and P(x)
why: A symbol phrase next to a word connective remains a conjunction.

id: unicode-symbols-inside-quantifier
context: nested
surface: ∀z, (z ∈ A ∧ P(z))
expect: forall z, (In(z, A) and P(z))
why: Symbols are rewritten before recursive parsing of the quantified body.
