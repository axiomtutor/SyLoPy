(*
A small end-to-end example: transitivity followed by symmetry, with both
assumptions discharged by nested conditional-introduction subproofs.
*)

# 1: Chaining an equivalence relation
## Proof that
### if R is an equivalence relation on X and a, b, c are in X.
### then R(a,b) -> (R(b,c) -> R(c,a)).

1. Let X be any set, R be an equivalence relation on X, and a, b, c be in X. (Declaration)
2. R(a,b) -> (R(b,c) -> R(c,a)). (Conditional Introduction from subproof below)
begin subproof
 2.1. R(a,b). (Assumption for Conditional Introduction)
 2.2. R(b,c) -> R(c,a). (Conditional Introduction from subproof below)
 begin subproof
  2.2.1. R(b,c). (Assumption for Conditional Introduction)
  2.2.2. R(a,c). (Relation Transitivity from 2.1, 2.2.1)
  2.2.3. R(c,a). (Relation Symmetry from 2.2.2)
 end subproof
end subproof
