# 1: Symmetry and transitivity give local reflexivity
## Proof that
### if R is symmetric and transitive and R(a,b)
### then R(a,a) and R(b,b).

1. Let X be any set, R be a symmetric, transitive relation on X, and a, b be in X. (Declaration)
2. R(a, b). (Premise)
3. R(b, a). (Relation Symmetry from 2)
4. R(a, a). (Relation Transitivity from 2, 3)
5. R(b, b). (Relation Transitivity from 3, 2)
6. R(a, a) and R(b, b). (Conjunction Introduction from 4, 5)

# 2: Transitivity composes a three-edge chain
## Proof that
### if R is transitive and R(a,b), R(b,c), R(c,d)
### then R(a,d).

1. Let X be any set, R be a transitive relation on X, and a, b, c, d be in X. (Declaration)
2. R(a, b). (Premise)
3. R(b, c). (Premise)
4. R(c, d). (Premise)
5. R(a, c). (Relation Transitivity from 2, 3)
6. R(a, d). (Relation Transitivity from 5, 4)

# 3: Transitivity has a useful contrapositive
## Proof that
### if R is transitive, R(a,b), and not R(a,c)
### then not R(b,c).

1. Let X be any set, R be a transitive relation on X, and a, b, c be in X. (Declaration)
2. R(a, b). (Premise)
3. not R(a, c). (Premise)
4. not R(b, c). (Proof by Contradiction from subproof below)
begin subproof
 4.1. R(b, c). (Assumption for contradiction)
 4.2. R(a, c). (Relation Transitivity from 2, 4.1)
 4.3. R(a, c) and not R(a, c). (Conjunction Introduction from 4.2, 3)
end subproof

# 4: A symmetric behavior premise conflicts with asymmetry
## Proof that
### if R is asymmetric and R(x,y) implies R(y,x)
### then not R(a,b).

1. Let X be any set, R be an asymmetric relation on X, and a, b be in X. (Declaration)
2. forall x, forall y, (R(x, y) -> R(y, x)). (Premise)
3. not R(a, b). (Proof by Contradiction from subproof below)
begin subproof
 3.1. R(a, b). (Assumption for contradiction)
 3.2. forall y, (R(a, y) -> R(y, a)). (Universal Instantiation from 2)
 3.3. R(a, b) -> R(b, a). (Universal Instantiation from 3.2)
 3.4. R(b, a). (Modus Ponens from 3.3, 3.1)
 3.5. not R(b, a). (Relation Asymmetry from 3.1)
 3.6. R(b, a) and not R(b, a). (Conjunction Introduction from 3.4, 3.5)
end subproof

# 5: Totality supplies the reverse direction when one direction fails
## Proof that
### if R is total and not R(a,b)
### then R(b,a).

1. Let X be any set, R be a total relation on X, and a, b be in X. (Declaration)
2. not R(a, b). (Premise)
3. R(a, b) or R(b, a). (Relation Totality from 1, 1)
4. R(b, a). (Proof by Cases from 3, subproofs below)
begin subproof
 4.1.case1. R(a, b). (Case)
 4.2.case1. R(b, a). (Explosion from 2, 4.1.case1)
end subproof
begin subproof
 4.1.case2. R(b, a). (Case)
 4.2.case2. R(b, a). (Reiteration from 4.1.case2)
end subproof

# 6: Symmetry makes a related pair bidirectional
## Proof that
### if R is symmetric and R(a,b) or R(b,a)
### then R(a,b) and R(b,a).

1. Let X be any set, R be a symmetric relation on X, and a, b be in X. (Declaration)
2. R(a, b) or R(b, a). (Premise)
3. R(a, b) and R(b, a). (Proof by Cases from 2, subproofs below)
begin subproof
 3.1.case1. R(a, b). (Case)
 3.2.case1. R(b, a). (Relation Symmetry from 3.1.case1)
 3.3.case1. R(a, b) and R(b, a). (Conjunction Introduction from 3.1.case1, 3.2.case1)
end subproof
begin subproof
 3.1.case2. R(b, a). (Case)
 3.2.case2. R(a, b). (Relation Symmetry from 3.1.case2)
 3.3.case2. R(a, b) and R(b, a). (Conjunction Introduction from 3.2.case2, 3.1.case2)
end subproof

# 7: A total antisymmetric relation has one orientation between distinct points
## Proof that
### if R is total and antisymmetric and a is not b
### then (R(a,b) or R(b,a)) and not (R(a,b) and R(b,a)).

1. Let X be any set, R be a total, antisymmetric relation on X, and a, b be in X. (Declaration)
2. not (a = b). (Premise)
3. R(a, b) or R(b, a). (Relation Totality from 1, 1)
4. not (R(a, b) and R(b, a)). (Proof by Contradiction from subproof below)
begin subproof
 4.1. R(a, b) and R(b, a). (Assumption for contradiction)
 4.2. R(a, b). (Conjunction Elimination from 4.1)
 4.3. R(b, a). (Conjunction Elimination from 4.1)
 4.4. a = b. (Relation Antisymmetry from 4.2, 4.3)
 4.5. (a = b) and not (a = b). (Conjunction Introduction from 4.4, 2)
end subproof
5. (R(a, b) or R(b, a)) and not (R(a, b) and R(b, a)). (Conjunction Introduction from 3, 4)

# 8: A four-cycle in a partial order identifies opposite vertices
## Proof that
### if R is a partial order and R(a,b), R(b,c), R(c,d), R(d,a)
### then a = c and b = d.

1. Let X be any set, R be a partial order on X, and a, b, c, d be in X. (Declaration)
2. R(a, b). (Premise)
3. R(b, c). (Premise)
4. R(c, d). (Premise)
5. R(d, a). (Premise)
6. R(a, c). (Relation Transitivity from 2, 3)
7. R(c, a). (Relation Transitivity from 4, 5)
8. a = c. (Relation Antisymmetry from 6, 7)
9. R(b, d). (Relation Transitivity from 3, 4)
10. R(d, b). (Relation Transitivity from 5, 2)
11. b = d. (Relation Antisymmetry from 9, 10)
12. a = c and b = d. (Conjunction Introduction from 8, 11)
